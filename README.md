## 📊 How It Works

The system uses advanced pose estimation to create a skeletal outline over your body. It detects your joints, including your wrists, elbows, and shoulders. As you perform a shooting motion, the software records the coordinates of these points to map your specific arc. 

The software compares your motion against ideal shooting mechanics stored in the database. When you release the basketball, the system captures the release angle and the height of your follow-through. It displays these metrics on the side panel of the application window.

## 💡 Tips for Best Results

*   Ensure you have plenty of light in your room. Artificial light or natural sunlight works well for body detection.
*   Wear contrasting clothing to help the camera distinguish your limbs from the background.
*   Keep the camera steady. Use a tripod or a flat surface to maintain a consistent angle.
*   Clear the clutter in the area behind you so the software focuses only on your movements.
*   Re-calibrate the camera settings if you move to a different room or change your distance from the screen.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Does the software record video?**
No, the system processes frames locally in your computer memory and discards them instantly. It only saves numerical data regarding your form.

**Can I use this with a phone camera?**
The current version works best with high-definition web cameras connected to your computer via USB.

**Does this track the basketball?**
The system prioritizes your body joints and arm motion. It uses your hand position to infer the release point but does not track the ball flight directly.

**How do I clear my session data?**
Open the Settings menu inside the application and select Clear History to delete past records.

**Is this software free?**
Yes, this project is open source and available for public use.
